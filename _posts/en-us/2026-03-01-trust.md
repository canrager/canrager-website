I've canceled my ChatGPT subscription due to a lack of trust in OpenAI's leadership. "We only trust AI, if we can trust its developers" says Judith Simon, deputy director of the German Ethics Council.<d-cite key="fias2024kitrust"></d-cite> The trustworthiness of global leading AI companies got challenged last week.

Anthropic, a global AI leader and the company behind "Claude", clearly draws the red line in two conditions: Fully autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance. 
<!-- They have kept this stand since starting collaborating with the U.S. military since XXXX. Citation. -->

Last week, the U.S. department of war gave Anthropic an ultimatum: Either give up on these points and promise unlimited cooperation or being punished with supply chain risk designation to stop all government contracts immediately (which historically has so far only been used for U.S. foreign adversaries.) Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei made clear that the company he leads will maintain its position. Meanwhile, U.S. President Trump and Secretary Hegseth started a defamation campaign against Anthropic on social media. I recommend watching <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MPTNHrq_4LU">this interview</a> for more details.<d-cite key="cbsnews2026amodei"></d-cite>

Just hours after Hegseth posted about his intent to stop the U.S. government from working with Anthropic, OpenAI announced their deal with the U.S. department of war on social media. OpenAI released an excerpt of their contract with the department of war.<d-cite key="openai2026dod"></d-cite> Their agreement clearly crosses the red lines that Anthropic set.

It is a clear warning signal to me that OpenAI leadership does not stand by a fellow American AI company when its democratic values are under threat. These developments strengthened my trust in Anthropic's leadership and broke my trust in OpenAI's leadership.

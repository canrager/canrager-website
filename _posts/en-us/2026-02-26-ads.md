ChatGPT's trustworthiness took a hit when introducing ads in January 2026.<d-cite key="openai2026chatgptads"></d-cite><d-cite key="theconversation2026chatgptads"></d-cite> 

Advertising in AI assistants is fundamentally different from advertising in search engines. When you search for "running shoes," your intent is clear and ads can match it. But in a conversation with an AI assistant, you share context you would never put into a search query, such as health concerns, financial worries, or career doubts.<d-cite key="techpolicypress2025aidad"></d-cite> U.S. Senator Ed Markey warned in an open letter to seven major AI companies that ads in chatbots pose unique manipulation risks: chatbots build human-like relationships with users, and advertising can be woven invisibly into the conversational flow, making it virtually impossible to distinguish genuine advice from sponsored content.<d-cite key="markey2026aichatbotads"></d-cite>

Both Anthropic and Google have taken the stance against ads. Anthropic published a clear commitment that Claude will remain ad-free.<d-cite key="anthropic2026spacetothink"></d-cite> Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis stated at Davos that Gemini has no plans for ads, reasoning that a truly helpful personal assistant must give recommendations that are unbiased and unadulterated.<d-cite key="axios2026geminiadfree"></d-cite>
